# Odisha Minor Mineral Concession Rules (OMMC), 2016 — Key Provisions

## Scope and applicability
The OMMC Rules 2016 regulate prospecting, mining, quarrying and transport of all
minor minerals in the State of Odisha. They apply to every person, firm, company
or government body that extracts or trades in minor minerals as notified under
the Mines and Minerals (Development and Regulation) Act, 1957.

## Minor minerals covered
Minor minerals under these rules include: ordinary earth, ordinary sand, gravel,
stone (building and road metal), murrum, boulder, shingle, kankar, brick-earth,
limestone, laterite, ordinary clay, decorative stone, and such other minerals as
notified by the State Government.

## Types of concessions
1. **Quarry Permit** — Short-term extraction right for small quantities (domestic
   or agricultural use). Issued by the Tahasildar.
2. **Quarry Lease** — A lease for systematic extraction over a defined area and
   period. Granted through auction by the competent authority.
3. **Mining Lease** — For large-scale or mechanised extraction. Requires a mining
   plan approved by the Director of Mines.
4. **Prospecting Licence** — Authorises searching for minor minerals before a
   lease is applied for.

## Eligibility and disqualification
An applicant for a lease must be an Indian citizen, a registered firm, or a
company incorporated in India. A person convicted of illicit mining, or one who
has defaulted on royalty or rent under any mineral concession, is disqualified
until the default is cleared.

## Lease area limits
The maximum area for a single quarry lease is 10 hectares for an individual and
20 hectares for a cooperative society or company. No person may hold more than
two leases in the same district.

## Lease period and renewal
A quarry lease is granted for a period not exceeding five years, and may be
renewed for a further period not exceeding five years at a time. Application
for renewal must be made at least 90 days before the existing lease expires.
The competent authority must dispose of the application before the lease term
ends; if not decided, the existing lease continues until orders are passed.

## Mining plan
Every lessee operating over an area of one hectare or more must prepare and
get approved a mining plan before commencing operations. The plan includes:
extraction methodology, annual production targets, environment management plan,
progressive mine closure plan, and reclamation strategy.

## Security deposit
The lessee must furnish a security deposit equal to the estimated royalty for
one year's planned production, or as specified in the auction terms, whichever
is higher. The deposit is adjusted against dues or forfeited on termination.

## Determination (termination) of lease
A lease may be determined by the competent authority if the lessee:
- Fails to pay royalty or dead rent within the notice period.
- Violates any condition of the lease.
- Contravenes any provision of the Act, Rules or a court order.
- Fails to commence work within six months of the grant.
Before determination, the lessee must be given a show-cause notice and an
opportunity to be heard.

## Illegal mining and penalties
Extraction, removal, transport or storage of minor minerals without a valid
concession is illegal mining. The competent authority may seize the mineral,
vehicle and equipment, levy penalty up to ten times the royalty value, and
initiate prosecution under Section 21 of the MMDR Act.
