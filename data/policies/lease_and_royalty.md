# Quarry / Mining Lease — Grant, Renewal, Royalty and Dead Rent

## Concession types
Under the Odisha Minor Minerals Concession (OMMC) framework, no person may carry
out prospecting, mining or quarrying of minor minerals except under a valid
concession: a prospecting licence, a quarry or mining lease, an auctioned source,
or a quarry permit. A limited exception exists for extraction by a person from
their own land for genuine agricultural or domestic use.

## Sairat sources
A sairat source is an identified minor-mineral source (for example a stone quarry
cluster or a riverbed sand ghat) recorded in the District Survey Report. Quarry
leases over such sources are typically granted to successful bidders by the
Tahasildar after the required statutory clearances are obtained.

## Renewal
An application for renewal of a mining or quarry lease must be made to the
competent authority at least 90 days before the lease expires, and such an
application should be disposed of before the existing term ends. Operators are
advised to track Environmental Clearance and Consent to Operate validity
separately, as a lease can be active while a dependent clearance has lapsed,
which blocks permit issue.

## Royalty and dead rent
The holder of a lease must pay royalty on the mineral despatched at the notified
rate, or dead rent, whichever is higher for the period. Where mineral is removed
in excess of a permitted quantity, the competent authority may recover the value
of the excess from the holder. If royalty or dead rent is not paid, the State
Government may issue notice requiring payment within the stipulated period, and
on continued default may take steps to determine (terminate) the lease and
forfeit the security deposit, after giving the holder an opportunity to be heard.

## Suspension
A lease may be shown as suspended when a statutory condition is unmet — for
example a lapsed clearance, non-filing of returns, or a pending enforcement
action. While suspended, fresh permits and passes cannot be issued against it.

## Statutory returns
Lessees must file periodic statutory returns reconciling production, dispatch and
stock. Returns are tracked as filed, pending, or overdue; overdue returns are a
common trigger for suspension and enforcement review.
